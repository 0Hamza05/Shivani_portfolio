# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: people who already know Shivani (friends, family, colleagues) and people who are meeting her professionally for the first time (potential employers, collaborators, media). Both groups arrive wanting to understand who she is, not just what she has done.

The experience should feel like being handed a well-made photobook and being told: take your time.

## Product Purpose

A life memoir in the form of a portfolio. Shivani's site documents her personal journey through "stages" — from school in India through university at Warwick, Deloitte, London life, Kathak dance, travel, and self-work — using photographs, video, and first-person writing. The stages are not a CV. They are a narrative of becoming.

Success looks like: a visitor finishes reading one stage and clicks into another. The site earns time, not just attention.

## Brand Personality

Warm, articulate, unhurried.

Shivani's own voice in the project descriptions is literary and reflective — it does not perform. The design should match that: confident enough not to need noise, warm enough to feel like a conversation, precise enough to signal craft.

## Anti-references

- Generic creative portfolios: dark backgrounds, neon accents, scroll-jacking, the Awwwards / Behance aesthetic. Feels like it's dressing up as a designer rather than being a person.
- Corporate LinkedIn-style: resume-formatted, achievement-led, transactional. Life reduced to credentials.
- Instagram-adjacent: grid-of-squares, heavy filters, influencer aesthetic. Lifestyle brand energy over personal narrative.

## Design Principles

1. **Story over showcase.** The photographs are evidence of a life, not deliverables. The design frames them as memory, not product.
2. **Warmth without sentimentality.** Intimate and personal, but never cloying. The writing is clear-eyed; the design should be too.
3. **Cultural rootedness is not a footnote.** Shivani's Indian heritage, Kathak background, and London life are not modifiers. They are load-bearing. The site should feel like it came from that specific life, not from a template.
4. **Earn the pace.** The site moves slowly on purpose. Animations breathe. Nothing rushes the visitor. This is a deliberate choice, not a performance budget problem.
5. **Presence over polish.** A slightly rough edge that feels true beats a slick surface that feels assembled. Authenticity is the visual strategy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Ensure all text over gradient or image backgrounds meets contrast requirements. Reduced-motion preference should pause animations (the parallax grid and marquee columns in particular). No known specific accommodations beyond standard web practice.
